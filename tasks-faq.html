Tasks & Assignment Specifications

Companion to the Fall 2026 Syllabus — full instructions and easy-to-grade rubrics for every graded task. The syllabus itself only summarizes each task in one line.

Weekly Missions use a fast 3-item checklist (well under 2 minutes to grade). The 3 Major Assignments use a compact 4–5-row analytic rubric with an XP value pre-assigned to each row.

Task Continuity FAQ

Can I reuse a previous Mission for this task?

Only when the task says so. Mission 5 explicitly asks you to reuse Mission 2, 3, or 4; Major 1 explicitly revises Missions 1, 2, and 4. Every other task is a fresh topic — though reusing the same underlying math concept across missions for your own coherence is always fine.

Does every task have to be different?

The tool and topic change each week; the deliverable format (a short artifact + a short note) stays deliberately similar, so you're never learning a new submission format — only new content.

Does it have to be math-related?

Yes, always. Every Mission, Major, and the Capstone must be built around a specific mathematics concept.

What grade level should I use?

Your choice — elementary, middle, or high school. This course defaults to middle/high school, but elementary is completely fine if you prefer it. State your chosen grade band in each submission.

Weekly Missions (M1–M7)

Detailed instructions and rubrics for the seven 30–60-minute weekly Missions. Each Mission is worth 30 XP, graded on the same 3-item checklist shape: Meets (10) / Partial (5) / Not Yet (0) per criterion. Submit to the Moodle dropbox named for the Mission.

Mission 1 — Toolkit Sandbox Warm-Up

Due: Week 1, Thursday · Time: ~30 min

Builds on: — fresh start, no prior work needed.

Objective: Get comfortable with GeoGebra, CODAP, and one GenAI tool before building anything graded.

Instructions:

  1. Create or log into a free GeoGebra and CODAP account.
  2. Build one simple object in GeoGebra (a triangle, a slider, or a point).
  3. Ask a GenAI tool one question about a common middle/high-school math misconception.
  4. Submit one screenshot of your GeoGebra object + the AI exchange + a 2–3 sentence reflection.
CriterionMeets (10)Partial (5)Not Yet (0)
Account setup & GeoGebra artifact present
AI question is math-specific; full reply pasted
Reflection connects both tools to teaching

Mission 2 — GeoGebra Micro-Build

Due: Week 2, Thursday · Time: ~45 min

Builds on: — independent; pick any concept.

Objective: Build one small dynamic-geometry object illustrating a single concept from your chosen grade band (elementary, middle, or high school).

Instructions:

  1. Pick a concept (e.g., slope, angle bisector, function transformation).
  2. Build a GeoGebra sketch with at least one slider or draggable point.
  3. Write a 2-sentence "what should a student notice" note.
CriterionMeets (10)Partial (5)Not Yet (0)
Sketch runs and illustrates the concept
At least one interactive element (slider/drag)
Noticing note is specific and accurate

Mission 3 — AI Worksheet Critique

Due: Week 3, Thursday · Time: ~45 min

Builds on: — independent; a new concept of your choice.

Objective: Practice directing AI rather than accepting its first answer.

Instructions:

  1. Prompt a GenAI tool to generate a short, two-tier differentiated worksheet for a concept of your choice.
  2. Check both tiers for mathematical accuracy.
  3. Write 3 specific edits you made (or would make) and why.

Edits show pedagogical reasoning, not just wording

Instructions:

  1. Pick a task you built in Mission 2, 3, or 4.
  2. Prompt AI for a scaffolded version and an extension version.
  3. Verify both mathematically; name one UDL principle each version reflects.

Mission 7 — Computational Thinking Mini-Task

Due: Week 10, Thursday · Time: ~45–60 min

Major 1 — Digital Toolkit Portfolio (150 XP)

Due: Week 6, Tuesday · Est. time: ~2–3 hours (built from Missions 1, 2 & 4, not from scratch)

Builds on: Missions 1, 2, and 4 — required; this task revises and compiles them.

Objective: Show growth across GeoGebra, CODAP, and AI-assisted design by revising and curating your best work so far.

Instructions:

  1. Choose your strongest artifact from Mission 2 (GeoGebra), Mission 4 (CODAP), and one AI-assisted piece (Mission 1 or 3).

Major 2 — AI Professional Ethics & Practice Portfolio (180 XP)

Due: Week 9, Thursday · Est. time: ~3 hours, built up across the semester (not written the night before)

Builds on: your cumulative AI Usage Log since Week 1, plus any prior AI-assisted artifact you choose to formalize.

This is the course's signature AI assessment — it exists specifically to show that a student can use GenAI as a math-teaching collaborator professionally, transparently, and ethically, not just competently.

Components (submit as one Moodle entry):

  1. AI Usage Log — a running table kept since Week 1: tool used · purpose · one key prompt · what you changed or verified afterward. Update it weekly rather than reconstructing it at the end.
  2. Annotated AI-Assisted Lesson Artifact — one lesson resource (activity, assessment, or task) that is clearly AI-co-created, with annotations showing exactly which parts came from AI, which you changed, and why.
  3. Professional Ethics Reflection (~1 page) answering:
    • a) One place the AI's output was wrong or biased, and how you caught it.
    • b) One ethical boundary you would set for your own future students' AI use.
    • c) How you would explain your AI-assisted planning to a parent or principal.

Rubric (180 XP total):

CriterionMeetsPartialNot Yet
AI Usage Log is complete and specific across the semester40200
Lesson artifact is instructionally sound and mathematically accurate40200
AI's contribution vs. your own editing is clearly, honestly annotated40200
Reflection identifies a real AI error/bias and how it was caught30150

Grading tip: the Usage Log is the easiest row to check fast — if it's empty or clearly backfilled in one sitting, that criterion is Not Yet regardless of how polished the rest looks.

Major 3 — Capstone: AI-Integrated Unit & Public Defense (180 XP)

Due: written unit plan Week 12, Thursday (Dec 10); live defense Week 13, Thursday (Dec 17) · Est. time: ~4–5 hours across Weeks 10–13

Builds on: the Weeks 10–12 backward-design work and the Week 11 Guild Critique peer feedback — required.

Objective: Independently design, and publicly defend, a short (2–3 lesson) AI-integrated mathematics unit that treats GenAI as a genuine collaborator.

Components:

  1. A unit plan: learning goals, lesson sequence, at least one GeoGebra resource and one CODAP-or-AI-generated resource.
  2. An AI-usage statement (per Syllabus Section 5).
  3. Incorporation of the peer feedback received at the Week 11 "Guild Critique."
  4. An 8–10 minute public defense/presentation with Q&A on Week 13, Thursday (Dec 17).

Rubric (180 XP total):

CriterionPoints
Unit coherence & standards alignment0–40
Integration quality of GeoGebra/CODAP0–35
AI used as collaborator — documented, ethical, verified use0–35
Incorporation of Week 11 peer feedback0–30
Defense: clarity + handling of Q&A0–40
Grading tip: score the written unit plan during the week between Dec 10 and Dec 17, then adjust only the "Defense" row live in the room — keeps grading during presentations to under a minute per student.
