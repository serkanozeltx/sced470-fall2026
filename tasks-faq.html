Task Continuity FAQ

Can I reuse a previous Mission for this task?

Only when the task says so. Mission 5 explicitly asks you to reuse Mission 2, 3, or 4; Major 1 explicitly revises Missions 1, 2, and 4. Every other task is a fresh topic — though reusing the same underlying math concept across missions for your own coherence is always fine.

Does every task have to be different?

The tool and topic change each week; the deliverable format (a short artifact + a short note) stays deliberately similar, so you're never learning a new submission format — only new content.

Does it have to be math-related?

Yes, always. Every Mission, Major, and the Capstone must be built around a specific mathematics concept.

What grade level should I use?

Your choice — elementary, middle, or high school. This course defaults to middle/high school, but elementary is completely fine if you prefer it. State your chosen grade band in each submission.

I'm a master's/graduate student — is the Capstone the same for me?

Talk to me in the first two weeks of the course. If you're pursuing a graduate degree, Major 3 (the Capstone) can be adapted into a paper or a component of your own research or manuscript instead of the standard K–12 unit plan. Missions and Majors 1–2 stay the same for everyone.

Should I submit screenshots or links for GeoGebra/CODAP/AI work?

Submit the tool's public share link whenever one is available, alongside or instead of a screenshot — a link lets me actually open and interact with your work. Before submitting, check it in a private/incognito browser tab to confirm it's really public and not just viewable while you're logged in.

I'm adding this course during add/drop — will I be penalized for missing Week 1?

No. See the Add/Drop Grace policy in Syllabus Section 3 — Week 1's Presence & Engagement points are simply left out of your denominator, not counted against you as zeros.

Weekly Missions (M1–M7)

Detailed instructions and rubrics for the seven 30–60-minute weekly Missions. Each Mission is worth 30 XP, graded on the same 3-item checklist shape: Meets (10) / Partial (5) / Not Yet (0) per criterion. Submit to the Moodle dropbox named for the Mission.

Mission1 — Philosophy of Technology Integration (Reflective Essay)

Due: Week 1, Thursday · Time: ~30–45 min

Builds on: — fresh start, no prior work needed. You'll revisit this exact essay in the very last week of the course.

Objective: Put into words, before any tool training begins, what you actually believe about technology's role in math teaching and learning. There are no right or wrong answers here — this is a starting point, not a test.

Instructions:

  1. Write a 300–500 word reflective essay on your personal philosophy of technology integration in mathematics education. Consider: What role should technology play in a math classroom? What excites you about it? What worries you? What experiences — as a student or observer — shaped this view?
  2. Be honest and specific rather than diplomatic. A genuinely uncertain or even skeptical essay is scored the same as an enthusiastic one — this is about depth of self-examination, not the "correct" stance.
  3. Submit your essay. Keep a copy for yourself — you'll be asked to reread and respond to it in Week 12, as a "bookend" to your Capstone.
CriterionMeets (10)Partial (5)Not Yet (0)
Essay is substantive and complete (300–500 words)
Reflects specific personal beliefs, not generic statements
Shows genuine self-examination, not a surface-level answer

Mission2 — GeoGebra Micro-Build

Due: Week 2, Thursday · Time: ~45 min

Builds on: — independent; pick any concept.

Objective: Build one small dynamic-geometry object illustrating a single concept from your chosen grade band (elementary, middle, or high school).

Instructions:

  1. Pick a concept (e.g., slope, angle bisector, function transformation).
  2. Build a GeoGebra sketch with at least one slider or draggable point.
  3. Write a 3–4 sentence "what should a student notice" note: what should they see happen as they drag or move the slider, and what mathematical idea does that reveal?

Submit your GeoGebra sketch's public share link (File → Share → Copy link), not just a screenshot — check the link works in a private/incognito tab first.

CriterionMeets (10)Partial (5)Not Yet (0)
Sketch runs and illustrates the concept
At least one interactive element (slider/drag)
Noticing note is specific and accurate

Mission3 — AI Worksheet Critique

Due: Week 3, Thursday · Time: ~45 min

Builds on: — independent; a new concept of your choice.

Objective: Practice directing AI rather than accepting its first answer — and see how a general-purpose model compares to a purpose-built teaching tool.

Instructions:

  1. Write one prompt asking for a short, two-tier differentiated worksheet for a math concept of your choice. Run that exact same prompt in both a general AI chat tool (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini) and coteach.ai.
  2. Check both tiers of both outputs for mathematical accuracy.
  3. Write a short comparison: how did the two outputs differ in accuracy, differentiation quality, and usefulness to you as a teacher? Then write 3 specific edits you made (or would make) to the version you'd actually use, and why.

Share the link/export of both AI conversations if the tool supports it — check it's viewable in a private/incognito tab before submitting.

CriterionMeets (10)Partial (5)Not Yet (0)
Both AI outputs included (general LLM + coteach.ai)
≥ 3 specific, correct edits identified, with a real comparison of the two tools
Edits show pedagogical reasoning, not just wording

Mission4 — CODAP Mini-Investigation

Due: Week 4, Thursday · Time: ~45 min

Builds on: — independent; a new dataset.

  3. Write one data-based claim and the specific evidence for it: what exactly in the graph supports your claim, and what would change your mind?

Mission5 — Differentiation Mini-Task

Due: Week 7, Thursday · Time: ~45 min

Builds on: Mission 2, 3, or 4 — required; pick one of those to extend.

Objective: Use AI to generate two tiers of one task, then verify them.

Instructions:

  1. Pick a task you built in Mission 2, 3, or 4.
  2. Prompt AI for a scaffolded version and an extension version.
  3. Verify both mathematically. For each version, answer: which UDL principle (representation, action & expression, or engagement) does it reflect, and specifically how?

Share the AI conversation link alongside your two versions if possible — check it's viewable in a private/incognito tab.

CriterionMeets (10)Partial (5)Not Yet (0)
Both tiers present and mathematically correct
UDL principle correctly named for each tier
Scaffolding/extension choices are pedagogically sound

Mission6 — AI Formative-Assessment Mini-Task

Due: Week 8, Thursday · Time: ~45 min

Builds on: — independent; a new concept of your choice.

Objective: Generate and vet a short formative check with AI.

Instructions:

  1. Prompt AI for a 3-question exit ticket on a concept of your choice.
  2. Solve all 3 yourself; flag and fix any AI errors.
  3. Write specifically what the results would tell a teacher to do next: if most students missed question 2, what's the concrete next instructional move?

Share the AI conversation link if possible — check it's viewable in a private/incognito tab.

CriterionMeets (10)Partial (5)Not Yet (0)
3 relevant, correctly-leveled questions
Your solutions are correct; AI errors caught
Next-step response is instructionally specific

Mission7 — Computational Thinking Mini-Task

Due: Week 10, Thursday · Time: ~45–60 min

Builds on: — independent; a new procedure.

Objective: Translate one mathematical procedure into an algorithmic, step-based form.

Instructions:

  1. Pick a procedure (e.g., long division, the quadratic formula).
  2. Write it as a numbered algorithm or a simple GeoGebra/CODAP script.
  3. Name one computational-thinking practice it uses — decomposition, abstraction, pattern recognition, or algorithm design (Weintrop et al., 2016).

If you build this in GeoGebra or CODAP, submit the public share link — check it's viewable in a private/incognito tab.

Major2 — AI Professional Ethics & Practice Portfolio (180 XP)

Due: Week 9, Thursday · Est. time: ~3 hours, built up across the semester (not written the night before)

Builds on: your cumulative AI Usage Log since Week 1, plus any prior AI-assisted artifact you choose to formalize.

This is the course's signature AI assessment — it exists specifically to show that a student can use GenAI as a math-teaching collaborator professionally, transparently, and ethically, not just competently.

Components (submit as one Moodle entry):

  1. AI Usage Log — a running table kept since Week 1: tool used · purpose · one key prompt · what you changed or verified afterward. Update it weekly rather than reconstructing it at the end.
  2. Annotated AI-Assisted Lesson Artifact — one lesson resource (activity, assessment, or task) that is clearly AI-co-created, with annotations showing exactly which parts came from AI, which you changed, and why. Share the AI conversation's public link alongside the artifact where possible.
  3. Professional Ethics Reflection (~1 page) answering:
    • a) One place the AI's output was wrong or biased, and how you caught it.
    • b) One ethical boundary you would set for your own future students' AI use.
    • c) How you would explain your AI-assisted planning to a parent or principal.

Major3 — Capstone: AI-Integrated Unit & Public Defense (180 XP)

Due: written unit plan Week 12, Thursday (Dec 10); live defense Week 13, Thursday (Dec 17) · Est. time: ~4–5 hours across Weeks 10–12, plus the Week 13 defense

Builds on: the Weeks 10–12 backward-design work and the Week 11 Guild Critique peer feedback — required. Also builds on Mission 1 for the Philosophy Bookend component (required — revisit and compare).

Pursuing a master's or other graduate degree? Talk to me in the first two weeks — this Capstone can be adapted into a paper or a component of your own research or manuscript instead of the standard K–12 unit plan.

Objective: Independently design, and publicly defend, a short (2–3 lesson) AI-integrated mathematics unit that treats GenAI as a genuine collaborator.

Components:

  1. A unit plan: learning goals, lesson sequence, at least one GeoGebra resource and one CODAP-or-AI-generated resource.
  2. An AI-usage statement (per Syllabus Section 5).
  3. Incorporation of the peer feedback received at the Week 11 "Guild Critique."
  4. A Philosophy Bookend (~1 page): reread your Mission 1 reflective essay from Week 1, then write about how — and whether — your philosophy of technology integration has changed across the semester. Be specific about what shifted and what didn't.
  5. An 8–10 minute public defense/presentation with Q&A on Week 13, Thursday (Dec 17).
